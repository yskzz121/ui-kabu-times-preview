Glimmers of an Iran War End — Pezeshkian: 'Necessary Will Exists'; Trump: 'Withdrawal in 2–3 Weeks'

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told the EU Council president in a March 31 phone call that he has "the necessary will to end this conflict," though he insisted on "guarantees to prevent a repeat of aggression" and continued to reject unconditional surrender. Meanwhile, President Trump told reporters at the White House that he would "pull out in two weeks, maybe three," suggesting that U.S. military objectives had been largely achieved. At the same time, he declared "we are completely unchecked — we bombed everything" and admonished allies to "go get your own oil." Five weeks after the Iran war began on February 28, the convergence of Trump's withdrawal signal and Pezeshkian's openness to dialogue triggered a sharp market rebound. The S&P 500 rallied +2.91% and the Dow surged 1,125 points in the biggest up day since May. However, Trump also warned that he would "totally destroy Iran's power facilities and oil wells" if no deal is reached, leaving the path to a ceasefire uncertain.

Crude Oil Posts Historic March — Brent's +63% Is Largest Monthly Gain Since 1988
March was a historic month for energy markets. Brent crude surged +63%, its largest monthly gain since records began in 1988. WTI rose +51%, the sharpest increase since May 2020. The effective blockade of the Strait of Hormuz due to the Iran war halted roughly 20% of the world's seaborne oil shipments. In the March 31 session, both benchmarks eased on peace hopes — WTI settled at $101.38 (-1.46%) and Brent at $105.13 (-1.56%) — but both remain at their highest levels since 2022. The average U.S. gasoline price has now breached $4 per gallon, amplifying the impact on consumers.
⚠️
Why It Matters: The historic surge in crude heightens the risk of renewed inflation and complicates the Fed's rate-cut outlook. It also directly raises transportation and manufacturing costs for businesses, raising concerns about Q2 earnings.

China and Pakistan Unveil 5-Point Peace Roadmap — Rare Mediation in U.S.-Led War
Pakistani Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met in Beijing and announced a five-point peace initiative for the Iran war. The proposal calls for an immediate ceasefire and humanitarian aid, the start of peace negotiations, a ban on use of force during talks, a halt to attacks on energy, water, and nuclear infrastructure, and the restoration of safe passage for civilian and commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz. It is highly unusual for China to step in as a mediator in a U.S.-led military operation. Pakistan is reportedly facilitating "structured message exchanges" between Washington and Tehran, albeit indirectly.

✍️ EDITORIAL

What '2–3 Weeks' Really Means — Decoding Trump's Withdrawal Signal and Pezeshkian's Conditions

Decoding Trump's withdrawal signal and Pezeshkian's conditions

On March 31, two critical statements on the Iran war moved markets. But caution is warranted. A close reading of both leaders' remarks reveals a path to ceasefire that is far more complicated than the surface impression suggests.

Trump's withdrawal signal: "Pull out in two, maybe three weeks" — this statement comes against the backdrop of shifting public opinion five weeks into the war. With U.S. gasoline prices surpassing $4 per gallon, even within the Republican Party, voices of concern are emerging ahead of the midterm elections. NPR reported that "Trump's mixed messages on the Iran war could have consequences for Republicans." At the same time, he continues to threaten to "totally destroy Iran's power facilities and oil wells" if no deal is reached — brandishing both the withdrawal and escalation cards simultaneously. This should be read as a negotiating tactic.

Pezeshkian's conditions: While President Pezeshkian told the EU Council president he has "the necessary will," he attached three conditions: (1) recognition of Iran's legitimate rights, (2) payment of reparations, and (3) international guarantees to prevent future aggression. Condition (2), the reparations demand, is extremely unlikely to be accepted by the U.S. and represents the biggest barrier to negotiations. Furthermore, PJ Media reported that "Pezeshkian is effectively being held 'hostage' by the IRGC (Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps) and is being blocked from direct negotiations with Trump." Whether the president's "will" aligns with the regime's "will" remains unclear.

Three scenarios:
Scenario A (Optimistic): Ceasefire agreement by mid-April. Strait of Hormuz reopens, crude drops to $80s. Probability: 20%
Scenario B (Base case): Partial ceasefire (temporary halt in attacks), with gradual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Crude falls to around $90. Probability: 50%
Scenario C (Bearish): Negotiations collapse, Trump carries out infrastructure strikes. Crude surpasses $130, VIX reaches 40s. Probability: 30%

Markets are currently pricing somewhere between Scenarios A and B, but may be underestimating the tail risk of Scenario C. The April 6 deadline concerning Kharg Island (Iran's primary oil export terminal) is also a key date to watch.

Silicon Photonics
Silicon photonics is a semiconductor technology that uses light (lasers) instead of traditional copper wiring to transmit data. By integrating optical waveguides, lasers, and photodetectors onto silicon substrates, it converts electrical and optical signals at high speed — dramatically accelerating and reducing the power consumption of server-to-server communication within data centers and AI training cluster interconnects. As the volume of data movement required in the AI era explodes, silicon photonics has emerged as the "next-generation interconnect technology" that breaks through the bandwidth and power consumption limits of copper wiring, making it one of the semiconductor industry's highest-priority focus areas.
💡
Why Light?
Copper wiring degrades signals over distance and generates more heat at higher speeds. Light has no electrical resistance and virtually zero signal degradation over long distances. A single optical fiber can carry hundreds of times more data than copper, making it essential for the ultra-high-speed data transfers required in large-scale AI training.
🏭
Industry Landscape
Beyond Nvidia's $2B investment in Marvell for joint development, Broadcom, Intel, and TSMC are all developing proprietary silicon photonics technologies. Aehr Test Systems surged 23% on orders for silicon photonics transceiver testing equipment, demonstrating investment flowing across the entire supply chain.
📊
Market Size
The silicon photonics market is projected to grow from approximately $2 billion in 2025 to over $8 billion by 2030, at a compound annual growth rate exceeding 30%. Demand from AI data centers is the single largest growth driver.
📌 Today's Connection The Nvidia-Marvell $2 billion partnership announced today centers precisely on joint development of silicon photonics. Nvidia's "NVLink Fusion" vision aims for a next-generation architecture that connects AI chips at ultra-high speeds via optical interconnects. MRVL's 12.8% surge is the market recognizing that this technology represents the semiconductor industry's next growth frontier.
