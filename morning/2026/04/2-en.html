Trump Delivers First Primetime Address — Declares War Will End 'Soon,' Hints at NATO Withdrawal

President Trump addressed the nation at 9:00 p.m. ET on April 1 in his first primetime speech on the Iran conflict, now in its 33rd day. "We are achieving an overwhelming victory and will soon accomplish all military objectives," he declared, adding that the U.S. would "strike very hard over the next two to three weeks." He maintained the April 6 deadline for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The most seismic moment came with his remarks on NATONATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization)A military alliance established in 1949 comprising 32 member nations including the U.S. Its cornerstone is the collective defense clause (Article 5), which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all.. Visibly frustrated that allied nations refused to join the Iran campaign, he called NATO a "paper tiger" and said he was "strongly considering" a U.S. withdrawal, adding "Putin knows it, too." Poland's defense minister responded with a call for calm, countering that "there is no NATO without America, no America without NATO." A 2023 law requires congressional approval for NATO withdrawal, but some legal scholars argue the president could circumvent it through executive authority, potentially triggering a constitutional showdown.

⚠️
Why it matters: A NATO withdrawal would upend 80 years of post-war security architecture. If realized, the implications for European defense, FX markets, defense stocks, and geopolitical risk premiums would be profound.
🔍 Our Take
The NATO withdrawal rhetoric is more likely a bargaining chip than a policy commitment. With the April 6 Strait of Hormuz deadline approaching, it reads as a tactical pressure play aimed at Europe. That said, the possibility of circumventing the 2023 law through executive authority cannot be dismissed, raising the specter of a legal confrontation. Market impact remains scenario-dependent, but the highest-probability outcome is still bluff.

ISM Manufacturing PMI at 52.7 — Expansion Holds, but Prices-Paid Index Spikes to 78.3, Stoking Inflation Fears
The March ISM Manufacturing PMI rose to 52.7 from February's 52.4, marking three consecutive months in expansion territory. Production climbed to 55.1 (+1.6pt) and new orders held steady at 53.5 (-2.3pt). But the headline-grabber was the prices-paid index: it surged 7.8pt to 78.3, the highest reading since June 2022. Over the past two months, the index has jumped 19.3pt, suggesting that the Iran-war-driven surge in energy costs is bleeding through to the broader manufacturing sector. The employment index remained in contraction at 48.7, raising the specter of a stagflationary dynamic — rising costs paired with declining payrolls.
⚠️
Why it matters: A prices-paid reading of 78.3 douses cold water on Fed rate-cut expectations. The simultaneous advance in manufacturing activity and price acceleration deepens the central bank's policy dilemma.

✍️ EDITORIAL

The Real Message Behind 'Paper Tiger' — Three Risk Scenarios From the NATO Rift

Three risk scenarios from the growing NATO rift

President Trump's characterization of NATO as a "paper tiger" and his suggestion of withdrawal in the April 1 address poses the most fundamental question to the post-war security order in 80 years.

Why NATO withdrawal, why now: The immediate trigger was European NATO members' refusal to join the Iran campaign and dispatch fleets to the Strait of Hormuz. For Trump, the optics of America spilling blood in the Middle East for energy security while Europe — the primary beneficiary — stands on the sidelines is untenable. Yet the rhetoric also carries a strong transactional flavor. With the April 6 Hormuz reopening deadline approaching, it can be read as a tactical pressure play aimed at European capitals.

Three risk scenarios:
Scenario 1 (Bluff — 50% probability): The comments are negotiating leverage and will not lead to formal withdrawal proceedings. Europe offers concessions (defense spending increases, Strait patrol participation), and the crisis subsides. Market impact is limited.
Scenario 2 (Partial disengagement — 30% probability): The U.S. scales back its NATO military commitments without formally withdrawing. European defense stocks (Rheinmetall, BAE Systems, etc.) surge, while U.S. defense names face uncertainty. Dollar weakens; euro strengthens.
Scenario 3 (Formal withdrawal — 20% probability): After a legal battle with Congress, the administration moves toward formal withdrawal. This triggers a fundamental reconfiguration of European security, shifts in Russia's geopolitical calculus, a sharp increase in defense spending, and erosion of confidence in the dollar's reserve-currency status. Markets sell off sharply.

The 2023 law requires congressional approval for NATO withdrawal, but the possibility of the president circumventing it through executive authority cannot be ruled out. Combined with the April 6 Hormuz deadline, this week represents a critical juncture for geopolitical risk.

📚 Today's Keyword
GLP-1 Receptor Agonist
GLP-1 Receptor Agonist
GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of drugs that mimic the action of GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1), a hormone secreted in the gut. Originally developed for type 2 diabetes, their pronounced appetite-suppressing and weight-loss effects have made them a "game changer" in obesity treatment, fueling explosive market growth. Injectable formulations — Ozempic (semaglutide, Novo Nordisk) and Zepbound (tirzepatide, Eli Lilly) — led the way, with combined global sales surpassing $50B in 2025.
💊
From Injectables to Oral
Foundayo (orforglipron), approved by the FDA today, is the first GLP-1 oral drug that can be taken once daily without food or water restrictions. It can reach patients who are averse to injections — estimated at more than 40% of the eligible population — dramatically expanding the addressable market.
💰
Market Size
The GLP-1 market is projected to reach $150B by 2030. Beyond the "Big Three" of Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, and Amgen, Pfizer, Roche, and others are vying for entry. The success of oral formulations is set to accelerate the pace of market expansion.
📊
Investment Impact
LLY's market capitalization is approaching $920B. Within the healthcare sector, GLP-1-related stocks are attracting institutional investor attention as "the next growth theme after AI." On the flip side, there are negative implications for obesity-adjacent food, beverage, and medical device makers.
📌 Today's Relevance Today's FDA approval of Foundayo marks a turning point as the GLP-1 market transitions from "injectable dominance" to the "oral era." LLY's +4.5% rally, pushing market cap toward $920B, reflects investors' recognition of the massive market expansion that oral GLP-1 drugs will unlock. The self-pay option starting at $149/month is a breakthrough in accessibility, making the drug available even without insurance coverage.
